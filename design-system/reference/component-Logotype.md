The wordmark is drawn, not set: three lowercase letters built on one circle, so the logo is identical in every environment and depends on no font licence.

## Construction

| | |
| --- | --- |
| x-height | 72 units |
| stem | 15 |
| bowl | 74.4 outer, 44.4 inner — a 15 monoline |
| overshoot | 1.2 units at baseline and x-height |
| ascender / descender | −26 / 98 |
| letter gap | 14 |

The overshoot is the detail that makes it look professionally drawn: a circle that stops exactly on the baseline reads smaller than a flat stem beside it, so the bowls run 1.2 units past on both sides.

The `e` aperture is cut at **45°** — the same angle as the mark's corner. One geometric idea used twice, which is why the wordmark and the mark read as one object instead of two.

## The mark, at two optical sizes

| Variant | Corners | Cut | Use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `qed-mark.svg` | 5.5% of the side, continuous-curvature smoothing | 46% | 32px and up |
| `qed-mark-16.svg` | square | 52% | 24px and below |

The corners are smoothed with cubic curves at a 0.70 handle ratio rather than circular arcs — a true squircle, so the curve never shows the flat spot a circular radius leaves where it meets the straight edge. At 16px that subtlety disappears and the cut goes with it, which is why the small variant exists.

## Lockup

Mark trailing the wordmark, bottom-aligned to the baseline, at 0.78× the x-height, gap 30 units. Lowercase always. `QED` in capitals appears only at the start of a sentence in prose.

Clear space: 7 units on every side.

## Never

Re-set the wordmark in a font. Stretch or condense it. Outline the mark. Remove the cut. Rotate it. Animate it as a spinner — the mark means a finished proof, and spinning it says the opposite. Use the large mark below 24px.
